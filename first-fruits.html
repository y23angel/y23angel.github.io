First Fruits: helping coaches see where they're needed

In 48 hours, our team built a web app for SEND Network that lets church planters track their own plan, and lets their coach, the Catalyst, step in at the right moment. I owned the Catalyst side, from layout to database access rules.

The problem

Coaching that lived in phone calls

A Catalyst coaches several church planters. Each planter's plan lived in verbal check-ins, so there was no shared record and no early signal when someone was struggling.

The client asked one question of the Catalyst home: "Where am I needed?" Not a dashboard of every number, but a short list of people to reach out to today.

What I built

The Catalyst journey

  • Garden home: greeting, a one-line summary, and "Where your presence helps" ranked by need.
  • Church review: church, pastor, tree stage by planting age, check-ins, goals, progress and conversation.
  • Check-in acknowledgement: the coach answers the latest check-in with a support note.
  • Category management: add, rename, reorder and safely delete objective categories.
  • Invitation status: pending, accepted and expired pastor invites.
  • Feature documentation for the team.

Design decisions

Small choices that kept it honest

Triage first, not every card equal

Churches that need attention move to the top, in a fixed order: support requested, then a check-in to review, then a check-in that is overdue.

Status from real activity

"Last activity" comes from progress entries and check-ins, not from someone editing a goal. A quiet church reads "may be time to reach out — not a health score."

A review must be a real review

A general reply under a goal doesn't clear a check-in. Only an acknowledgement linked to that exact check-in does, so no planter falls off the list by accident.

No false all-clear

If churches fail to load, the page shows an error and hides the counts. It never says "everyone is up to date" when it simply couldn't check.

Privacy by assignment

A Catalyst sees only churches assigned to them. Another coach's church, or another organization's, returns "not found", enforced on the server, not just hidden in the UI.

Protect people's history

A category with goals can be renamed but not deleted, and the prayer category can't be deleted, so no planter loses their record.

How it came together

Working in a fast-moving team

I also coordinated the team's merge order. When two pull requests built the same invitation page, I found the conflict, wrote it up, and helped the team settle on one flow.

Code review loop. My main PR went through two rounds of review. The reviewer asked for assigned-church scoping, real status data, and separating check-in reviews from ordinary replies. I made each change, added tests, and rebased onto the new baseline three times without breaking teammates' work.

The access rule I wrote, "a Catalyst only sees assigned churches", became the model the team used for the shared prayer and support conversations.

Under the hood

Built to be trusted

  • Next.js App Router with server components and server actions
  • Supabase Postgres with row-level security; the author and organization always come from the session, never the form
  • A SQL migration linking acknowledgements to check-ins
  • Unit and component tests for every status rule, permission check and form state (Vitest, Testing Library)
  • Accessible forms: visible labels, focus styles, text badges (not colour alone), errors that keep what you typed

What I learned

Design is also what you refuse to show

The most important design work here was deciding what not to say: no health scores, no false all-clear, no data a coach shouldn't see.

I also learned to work in public: small pull requests, clear notes to reviewers, and checking teammates' branches before force-pushing.
